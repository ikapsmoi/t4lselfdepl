# Market Prioritization

This roadmap prioritizes the business lines that have the strongest combination of real demand potential, product fit, and conversion value in the Travel4Life portfolio.

## Prioritization framework

Each cluster is scored on four criteria:

- Commercial intent: Is the user ready to enquire or book?
- Product fit: Does Travel4Life genuinely serve the trip type or destination?
- Content opportunity: Can the page provide real value beyond generic marketing?
- Conversion plausibility: Can the user move quickly from research to a quote or enquiry?

Scores are qualitative, not guaranteed forecast models.

## Tier 1 — highest priority

### 1) Tomorrowland travel planning from India

Why it matters:
- Strong commercial intent.
- Clear need for planning, cost guidance, and destination comparison.
- Travel4Life can serve as a planning and enquiry layer without pretending to be the organiser.

Target pages:
- `/tomorrowland/`
- `/tomorrowland/belgium/`
- `/tomorrowland/winter/`
- `/tomorrowland/thailand/`
- `/tomorrowland/packages/`

### 2) Jim Corbett leisure and resort package intent

Why it matters:
- High-volume destination-and-trip search intent.
- Family, couple, weekend, and group stay searches align with the site's working travel offerings.
- Can support both direct booking intent and custom package enquiries.

Target pages:
- `/jim-corbett/`
- `/jim-corbett/resorts/`
- `/jim-corbett/weekend-packages/`
- `/jim-corbett/safari-resorts/`

### 3) Corporate MICE and retreat planning

Why it matters:
- B2B leads often have higher value and strong commercial intent.
- Existing search categories such as offsite, retreat, team outing, and destination meeting planning are valuable.

Target pages:
- `/mice/`
- `/jim-corbett/corporate/`
- `/jim-corbett/mice/`

## Tier 2 — strong but should be content-led

### 4) Destination wedding planning in Jim Corbett

Why it matters:
- Wedding-related queries are highly commercial, but the business must avoid over-claiming on venue and planning control.
- A clear planning and enquiry model is a natural fit.

Target pages:
- `/weddings/`
- `/jim-corbett/weddings/`

### 5) Europe discovery and budget planning from India

Why it matters:
- Good potential for international trip intent.
- Needs a real inventory or planning-first strategy to avoid thin, low-value pages.
- The current architecture uses a strong first hub and then grows only when live product relevance exists.

Target pages:
- `/europe/`
- `/europe/tours-from-india/`
- `/europe/tour-cost/`
- `/europe/tours-under-200000/`

## Tier 3 — opportunistic or conditional

### 6) Niche route or destination pages

Examples:
- country pages (Switzerland, France, Italy)
- luxury or honeymoon segments
- city or route pages with no direct evidence or inventory support

These are worth adding only when:
- there is actual product relevance,
- the content clearly differs from the main hub,
- and the page can support a real enquiry workflow.

## Decision framework for new market pages

Add a new market or destination page only when it checks all of the following:

1. There is meaningful query intent or observed demand.
2. Travel4Life can support the destination or traveller type realistically.
3. The page offers a differentiated user promise.
4. The page has a clear enquiry or quote CTA.
5. The page avoids unsupported claims on dates, prices, or partnership status.

## Recommended market sequence

1. Tomorrowland planning pages
2. Jim Corbett leisure and weekend pages
3. Corporate MICE pages
4. Wedding planning pages
5. Europe hub and budget pages
6. Additional country/city/route pages as evidence and product support improve

## Market caution

Do not assume every destination or traveller segment should become its own page. The smart growth path is to prioritize pages with actual business fit, measured demand, and a clear conversion lead. In this project, that means strong destination hubs and segment pages supported by real user intent, not large volumes of low-value ranking pages.
